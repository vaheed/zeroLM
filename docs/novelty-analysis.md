# Novelty analysis

Status: revision 1, 2026-11-26. Written to decide whether ZeroLM can make a contribution that is more than an engineering reimplementation. Every reference below was located through a web search on 2026-11-26 and the arXiv or publisher record was seen; entries marked *not verified* were not. No claim of novelty is made here. This file says where a claim could be made and what would have to be shown.

## What the Gate A rerun does and does not show

The rerun (probe gain 1.55 BPB (bits per byte), held-out 2.71 BPB, retention 0.95) shows that single-pass online truncated BPTT (backpropagation through time) on a 20,000-parameter GRU (gated recurrent unit) learns useful byte features from one pass over 10^7 bytes. That is the NNCP route (Bellard, lossless compression with online-trained neural networks) and the dynamic-evaluation route (Krause et al.). It is feasibility of a known method. It is also 0.6 BPB behind Kneser-Ney order 5. The e-prop (eligibility propagation) failure (0.010 BPB probe gain against 1.368 for the gradient control) is a negative result for that one local rule at that scale, and stays on record in `research/decisions/gate-a-2026-11-26.md` and `research/decisions/gate-a-evidence/`.

## Where a defensible contribution could lie

1. **Not** in individual mechanisms: match models, caches, fast weights, Hopfield retrieval, online gradient learning, and compiled transformers each exist.
2. **Possible**: a *compiled algorithmic initialization that remains trainable* gives better data efficiency than random initialization, and than hand-coded fixed components, at equal parameters, data, and compute, on tasks where the algorithm is the bottleneck. Nobody found in this search has measured that comparison for language modelling from scratch. This absence is from a bounded search, not a proof.
3. **Possible, weaker**: an auditable, reversible, hash-pinned patch format for edits to a learner's weights. This is engineering and governance, not a modelling advance, unless validated patches are shown to add capability.

## Experiment N0 (run): is there headroom for the induction prior on bytes?

Before building anything larger, `zerolm-headroom` measures the ceiling of the best-known compiled prior (match model) over Kneser-Ney 5 and PPM-C 5 on the **development** role (hidden role untouched), paired block bootstrap, 95% intervals.

| exposure bytes | fiction gain over KN-5 (v2 mixer) | non-fiction gain over KN-5 (v2 mixer) |
|---|---|---|
| 10^5 | 0.120 [0.112, 0.129] | 0.355 [0.337, 0.376] |
| 10^6 | 0.044 [0.038, 0.051] | 0.227 [0.212, 0.244] |
| 10^7 | 0.011 [0.004, 0.016] | 0.148 [0.136, 0.161] |

Two things follow. (i) The gain shrinks as exposure grows, because a large count model already contains most repeats; on fiction it is about 0.01 BPB at 10^7 bytes, one fiftieth of the 0.6 BPB gap that the GRU must close. (ii) The v1 mixer (a bucket-average confidence) *lost* up to 0.09 BPB on fiction at 10^6-10^7 bytes; v2 (confidence conditioned on the count model's own probability of the predicted byte) was written after seeing v1 on the development role. Both are kept in `research/decisions/novelty-n0-evidence/`. Consequence: held-out BPB cannot be the primary metric for the induction prior; a conventional hybrid (arm H) captures what there is.

## Rules for the claims in this repository

Combining existing methods is not novelty. A novelty claim needs the decision in `docs/experimental-design.md` and a reproducible win over arms E, G, and H. Until then the project describes itself as engineering. See `docs/research-hypotheses.md`.
